# HIDDEN USES

**Episode:** Hidden Uses #1 — agent
**Frame:** 9:16, 1080×1920, 30 fps, 1500 frames
**Duration:** 50.0 seconds

## Logline

A dish-soap bottle gets the word stamped on it, three stranger uses of *agent* light up as hotspots down one kitchen table, and a three-second guess holds back the use that can make you sick.

The format is one everyday scene. The word is already in the room. Unusual uses ignite on the objects that actually do them. This episode teaches four uses of one word: cleaning agent, wetting agent, grammatical agent, biological agent.

Literary agent is real — a person who represents a writer to publishers — and it stays out of this cut. It is the ordinary “person who acts for someone else,” which the coat already stands for, and a fifth chunk would break the 3–5 chunk cap.

## The four chunks

Nothing else is taught. No etymology, no pronunciation aside, no software, no second word.

| # | Chunk | Where it plays | What the hotspot shows |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Cleaning agent | 8.00–14.50 | Dish soap lifting a grease smear off a plate |
| 2 | Wetting agent | 14.50–21.00 | A beaded drop on a waxy leaf spreading into a sheet |
| 3 | Grammatical agent | 21.00–28.00 | Sam staying the doer when the sentence goes passive |
| 4 | Biological agent | 31.00–39.00 | Abstract dots on the cutting board, after the pause |

Chunks 1–3 are the evidence. Chunk 4 is the answer to the question asked at 0:00. The spy coat is the wrong guess, not a fifth chunk.

## Storyboard

One locked table, shot from a high three-quarter angle. Objects sit in a vertical column so the frame reads top to bottom: soap, leaf, cookbook, cutting board. The camera never dollies to a new set. A “new shot” is a scale punch and a palette change. Safe area: rings and the word stay inside the center 70% width, below the top 140 px and above the bottom 280 px.

Timings sum to 50.00 s.

### 0.00–2.00 Hook (2.00 s)

Macro on a blank dish-soap label. Warm practical light. A trench-coat silhouette is a soft blur on the left edge, with no face and no props. At 0.40 the word **AGENT** scales onto the label with a short overshoot and locks by 1.30. Three unlit rings sit down the frame, on the leaf, the cookbook, and the cutting board. No logo, no series card, no intro.

Curiosity gap, spoken: “Which agent on this table can make you sick?” The surprising claim is visual and simultaneous: the word is on the soap, and the coat cannot take focus.

### 2.00–8.00 Wrong guess (6.00 s)

At 2.00 the grade drains to noir and the coat snaps sharp. **AGENT** slides off the lapel and falls. By 5.20 the grade snaps back to warm and the full column is on screen: soap (upper third), waxy leaf and a glass dropper (middle), open cookbook (lower middle), bare cutting board (bottom). The coat shrinks to a grey pin in the top-left corner and never grows a ring.

Re-hook, ending at 8.00: “Find the one that can make you sick.”

### 8.00–14.50 Chunk 1 — cleaning agent (6.50 s)

At 8.00 an amber ring ignites on the bottle and the frame punches in. A grease smear crosses a white plate; suds wipe through it; the smear lifts. Hold the clean streak from 11.20 to 12.40. Static label “cleaning” sits on the bottle; **AGENT** is the only moving type.

Re-hook, ending at 14.50: “It still can’t make you sick.”

### 14.50–21.00 Chunk 2 — wetting agent (6.50 s)

At 14.50 the palette shifts amber to cyan and the frame punches in on the leaf. The soap ring dims to a memory dot. From 14.50 to 16.30 plain water sits on the waxy leaf as a bead. At 16.40 a drop from the glass dropper hits and spreads into a sheet along the leaf. Hold the sheet from 18.80 to 20.00. The dropper is not the dish soap, and it carries no brand.

Re-hook, ending at 21.00: “Still not the one.”

### 21.00–28.00 Chunk 3 — grammatical agent (7.00 s)

At 21.00 the column gives way to a paper-white cookbook page in magenta. The sentence fades in as static type, not letter-by-letter motion: “Sam cut the onion.” The ring and **AGENT** land on Sam. At 22.30 that line fades out and the passive fades in as a second static line: “The onion was cut by Sam.” The magenta ring stays on Sam. At 24.60 a grey bar marks “The onion,” in time with the voice. Hold that split through 26.60.

Re-hook, ending at 28.00: “None of these can make you sick.”

### 28.00–31.00 Retrieval pause (3.00 s)

At 28.00 the picture dims. Two equal grey cards stack in the center: a suit silhouette labeled “suit,” and seven plain dots labeled “germ.” Center caption: **PAUSE**. Voice says “Pause.” and stops by 28.40. Silence from 28.40 to 31.00 (2.60 s). Music is nearly out. No ring lights, no answer.

### 31.00–39.00 Chunk 4 — biological agent, the answer (8.00 s)

At 31.00 the suit card greys, takes an X, and drops off the bottom. The dots fall onto the cutting board and a green ring ignites. **AGENT** locks beside the static word “biological.” The definition runs 31.10–35.20, slow on “biological agent,” and the lit board holds 35.20–35.80. The green ring stays up through 39.00.

Re-hook at 35.80–36.70: “And the coat?” The grey pin twitches and stays dark through 39.00.

The dots are flat circles. No species name, no equipment, no procedure.

### 39.00–45.00 Payoff (6.00 s)

At 39.00 the full column returns. The four rings pulse in order while the voice names them: soap, leaf, Sam, then the germ, held long enough to restate the answer. The coat pin stays grey. The open question is closed here in speech and picture: the germ is the one that can make you sick, and it has already been named as a biological agent.

Re-hook, 43.00–43.80: “Count them again.” The four rings then hold to 45.00.

### 45.00–50.00 Loop (5.00 s)

The four rings collapse into the bottle. The label clears. The coat returns to the left-edge blur. The last frame equals the first frame: blank label, coat out of focus, warm macro, before the stamp. No second reading of the question inside this file. “Count them again” finishes at 43.80. The collapse carries that line onto the opening frame. On a platform loop the next words are “Which agent on this table can make you sick?”

## Visual system and animation style

Flat cookbook illustration: thick outlines, few colors, no 3D, no character acting. The coat is a silhouette. Suds are a soft amber wipe. The water sheet is a circle that eases into an ellipse along the leaf vein, not a fluid simulation. The sentence change is a fade between two static lines.

Palette, one hue per chunk, used for the ring, the label, and the grade:

- Cleaning: amber
- Wetting: cyan
- Grammatical: magenta on paper white
- Biological: green
- Coat and suit: grey, never a ring color

Type: one heavy grotesque. **AGENT** is the only kinetic type (scale, overshoot, and a move from object to object). Every other on-screen word is static, short, and parented to its object, not parked in a bottom caption bar. The cookbook sentences are the diagram. They are not a transcript of the narration.

Motion inside each beat changes about every 1.5–3 s (grade, punch-in, wipe, fade, card). Each hotspot holds its result about 1.2 s after the action finishes. A tick marks each ring ignition. Music is a quiet plucked bed, ducked under the voice, and nearly gone from 28.40 to 31.00. One warm human voice. Slow down only on the word *agent*.

Mute pass: soap labeled **AGENT** plus “which one?”; coat with the word sliding off and “not the spy”; grease lifting under “cleaning **AGENT**”; bead becoming a sheet under “wetting **AGENT**”; Sam ringed while “the onion” is marked “subject”; **PAUSE** between “suit” and “germ”; X on the suit and “biological **AGENT**” on the dots; four rings; collapse back to the blank soap.

## What the uses mean

Checked so the hotspots teach real uses, not nicknames.

- **Cleaning agent.** A cleaning product is a liquid, powder, spray, or granule that removes dirt and grease. CDC lists liquid soap and detergents as cleaning agents. The hotspot is the soap lifting grease, not a person scrubbing.
- **Wetting agent.** A surfactant that lowers the surface tension of water so it spreads on a hard-to-wet surface instead of beading. That is the KRÜSS glossary sense, and it is why agricultural spreaders are added to sprays on waxy leaves (Texas A&M and Purdue extension). The demo is one houseplant leaf. No product rate, no second example.
- **Grammatical agent.** The doer of the verb, a thematic role. In a passive, the doer can leave the subject slot. “The onion was cut by Sam” still has Sam as the agent and the onion as the subject (*Essentials of Linguistics*, thematic roles and passives). Sam is animate on purpose. The soap is not called a grammatical agent.
- **Biological agent.** WHO Laboratory Biosafety Manual, 4th edition: a microorganism, virus, biological toxin, particle, or other infectious material that may cause infection, allergy, toxicity, or another hazard to people, animals, or plants. The line “can be a microorganism, a virus, or a toxin” is a true short list, not the full WHO wording. The picture is seven plain dots. No names, no method, no weapon.

## Series plan

Named, numbered series: **Hidden Uses**. Episode 1 has no title card, because the first two seconds are the hook and the last frame has to match the first. From episode 2 on, each film opens with a three-second card labeled **Hidden Uses check** before its own hook, so the callback is part of the episode and not a vague Easter egg. The check quizzes one or two earlier words, then answers on screen.

1. **Hidden Uses #1 — agent.** This film.
2. **Hidden Uses #2 — solution.** A diner glass of salt water; hotspots on the dissolved salt, a saline bag, and rubber cement, with the familiar “the answer” left dark. Check #1: coat or soap — which one is the agent?
3. **Hidden Uses #3 — bond.** A stuck honey jar; hotspots on the sugar sealing the lid, the chemical bonds in the glass, and a bail-bond receipt. Check #1 and #2: “cleaning ___” and “salt water is a ___.”
4. **Hidden Uses #4 — culture.** A yogurt tub; hotspots on the live culture in the cup, a blood-culture vial, and cells in a dish, with a museum postcard left dark. Check #2 and #3: “salt water is a ___” and “the jar seal is a ___.”
5. **Hidden Uses #5 — vessel.** A saucepan; hotspots on the pot, a vein at the wrist, and a ship. Check #3 and #4: “the jar seal is a ___” and “yogurt is a ___.”

## Production approach

**Remotion, difficulty medium.**

The film is a 50.0-second vertical timeline with one voice track, labels parented to objects, and a last frame that must equal the first. Remotion is built for that: React components, frame-accurate `interpolate` / spring motion, and a burned-in picture at 1080×1920. The art is flat SVG. Three.js would add a camera and a lighting rig this locked table does not use. Manim’s blackboard look fights the kitchen. No mix.

Medium, not low, because four synced hotspots, the passive fade, the silent pause, and the frame-matched collapse have to land on the timestamps above. Not high, because there is no 3D, no fluid sim, and no character animation. The water sheet is a circle easing into an ellipse.

## What this format refuses to be

Hidden Uses refuses to be a definition card: if a use is not happening on the object in frame, it does not ship.

## Measurement

Judge the cut on the share still watching at 2 s, average percentage viewed, the retention curve, and saves and sends per reach; an end-of-curve rewatch spike means the loop is working. Ignore raw view counts, and do not ask anyone to share.

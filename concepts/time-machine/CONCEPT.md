# TIME MACHINE — agent

**Series:** Time Machine #1
**Frame:** 9:16 (1080×1920), 30 fps
**Duration:** 50.0 seconds (1500 frames). Frame 0 and frame 1499 are the same picture.
**Word:** agent
**Spine:** a vertical timeline. One wheel stays in frame; what turns it changes at each stop, and the meaning changes with it.

## Logline

A timeline drops through *agent*, from Latin *agere* to the deputy people picture, and withholds which meaning came first until a three-second pause is over.

## What the format refuses to be

It refuses to be a definition card, a myth told and then corrected, or a software demo.

## Visual system and animation style

Vertical rail, left side, inset to 8% of the width so it clears the edge. The scene is full-bleed behind it. Four still-life stops, one object: a wheel. The cause of the turn is the meaning.

- **now** — peach hallway, keys and a contract, practical light.
- **Latin** — ochre dust, a hand and a cart wheel.
- **before 1500** — amber, crank and a clouding vessel.
- **1550s** — blue-grey storm light, wave and foam; the hand stays small in the back.
- **1590s** — green-black, a seal, a letter, a second person.
- **quiz** — chalk white, stamps, no camera move.
- **payoff** — back to ochre and the single hand.
- **loop** — pixel match of frame 0.

AGENT is the only kinetic type. It sits on the optical center (about 48% down), cap height about 11% of the frame, inside the middle 60% of the width. Per-letter motion is limited to that word: a kick on the hook, a forward shear when the wheel is pushed, a heavier weight when the vessel clouds, a thin blur when the wave hits, a gap opening and closing when the deputy steps in, a stillness during the pause, a lock on the payoff. Captions are burned in, hard-cut, two to five words, at about 70% down. They do not animate letter by letter. Date ticks on the rail are static. Nothing important sits in the bottom 18% or the right 16% (platform controls). The carved *agere* is scenery on the cart, small, still.

Sound: a warm conversational voice, slowed on the word *agent*. Score ducked under the voice. One soft tick as each rail stop locks. No tick under the pause. The opening chord returns at 45.5.

## Dates

No year in the film was invented. On-screen ticks are the bounds below.

- Latin *agere*: "to set in motion, drive forward; to do, perform." *Agens* / *agentem*: present participle, "effective, powerful." No calendar year is claimed. [Etymonline, agent](https://www.etymonline.com/word/agent)
- English, late 15th century, "one who acts" (Etymonline). OED's earliest evidence is before 1500, in George Ripley; the film says "before 1500" and does not name him, and it does not use 1471. The 15.0 line follows the Oxford Learner's gloss for that stage, someone or something that produces an effect. Merriam-Webster's first-known-use note is also the 15th century, for "one that acts or exerts power," and its Middle English gloss is "force capable of acting on matter." The payoff's "doer" is Etymonline's "one who acts," the same early stage, not a second date.
- 1550s: Etymonline dates "any natural force or substance which produces a phenomenon" to the 1550s. The line says "recorded as," because the 15th-century glosses already include a force that acts. The hand stays in the shot so the picture does not pretend persons dropped out.
- 1590s: English "deputy, representative" (Etymonline). Merriam-Webster's etymology also glosses Medieval Latin *agens* as "something capable of producing an effect, person authorized to act for another." The film does not claim the authorized-person idea was born in the 1590s. It claims the English deputy sense is the later English attestation.
- OED also derives the English word partly from French *agent* and partly from Latin. There is no French stop; that would be a fifth chunk and a date this script will not guess.
- Left off on purpose: "spy, secret agent," attested by 1916 (Etymonline), and every computing sense. The shift a fluent speaker is likely to miss is the English order: doer before deputy.

## Series plan

**Time Machine.** Numbered. No title card inside #1 (the hook cannot wait for one). The name is spoken in the cold open of #2–#5, which is a three-second quiz of earlier episodes, then that episode's own timeline. The quiz names the series and the earlier word, asks, holds, then answers, so the link is the lesson and not a "watch next" tag.

1. **agent** — this film. Latin *agere*, "to do," through an English doer (before 1500), a force or substance (1550s), to a deputy (1590s).
2. **silly** — Old English *gesælig* meant happy, fortunate; the foolish sense is attested by the 1570s. Cold open quizzes #1: "Time Machine one. Agent: doer, or deputy?" Answer after a short hold: "Doer. Before 1500."
3. **meat** — Old English *mete* meant food in general; animal flesh is the narrower sense from about 1300. Cold open quizzes #1 and #2: agent (doer or deputy?) and silly (happy or foolish first?).
4. **clue** — a *clew*, a ball of thread you could follow, before it was a hint. Cold open quizzes #2 and #3: silly and meat.
5. **broadcast** — seed thrown widely by hand (1767) before a radio signal (1922 as adjective and noun). Cold open quizzes #4 and #1: clue (thread or hint?) and agent (doer or deputy?).

## Production

**Remotion + React Three Fiber. Difficulty: medium.**

Remotion is the clock: 1500 frames, captions, the kinetic word, the ducked score, the 3.0s silence, and the matched first and last frames. Each stop is a still life in React Three Fiber (wheel, crank, wave plane, letter), rendered on that same frame clock and cut on the rail ticks. Three.js alone makes burned-in captions and a sample-accurate pause clumsy; Remotion alone flattens the wheel gag into crossfades. No character rigs, no fluid sim (the wave is a textured plane), no screen UI. Medium, because two renderers have to share one clock and the silence has to sit on the voice waveform, not because the scenes are long.

## Measurement

Judge the cut by the share still watching at 2 seconds, average percentage viewed and the shape of the retention curve, engaged-view rate, and saves and sends per reach — not by raw views.

# Clamp

**Format name:** Clamp

**Logline:** One word sits in a vertical slot. Each real sentence is a metal clamp that snaps shut and forces a single picture — and only one meaning — into the letters.

**Episode:** Clamp #1 — agent

**Video:** 9:16, **46 seconds**. Timings below sum to 46.0.

The same word, *agent*, is dropped into four sentence frames. The frame decides the meaning. Real estate, sports, and grammar all lock a person. The chemistry frame does not. That is the whole film.

## Visual system and animation style

Locked 1080×1920 stage. No camera move. The ground is matte charcoal for the full 46 seconds, so a color change inside the slot reads as a new shot.

**The slot.** A tall rounded rectangle, optically centered, about 42% of the width and 50% of the height. It sits above the platform UI: essential type stays out of the top 220px and the bottom 380px. The picture never leaves the slot. There is no room to walk around, and no pins or labels on a scene.

**The clamp.** Two blank steel bars, one above the slot and one below, slide in and meet with a short overshoot. They carry no words. A clamp tick marks the contact. The bars are the sentence locking; the words of the sentence stay in the voice.

**The word.** *agent* is the only kinetic type. It lives in the center of the slot, about 70% of the slot width, lowercase, heavy, high contrast. On every lock it does the same move: scale 1.12 → 1.00 with a 4% overshoot, and the letter fill swaps to that frame’s color (brass, turf green, ink blue, then clear liquid). Captions do not move, bounce, or track.

**Captions.** One short line, static, directly under the slot and above the bottom safe zone. Same-language English, burned in, cut with the beat. They are not a transcript of the voiceover. Sound off, the picture plus *agent* plus that short line still tells the frame.

**Inside the slot, one object at a time:**

- House: a brass key, bow cut like a gable, half-turned in a black front-door lock. A hand cropped at the wrist. Amber light.
- Free agent: a green jersey, number 7, seen at the shoulder. A silver chain snaps off the collar. Turf-green light.
- Grammar: a white vase with one crack, and a hand with a chip of porcelain on the thumb. *agent* is stamped on the knuckles. One ink arrow goes from the knuckles to the vase. Paper-white light.
- Oxidizing: a clear bottle pours into the letterforms. Behind them, a square of white cloth with a dark berry stain. A faint person-shaped silhouette sits in the counters of the letters until the reveal rinses it out and the stain goes pale. Glass-blue light.

**Motion inside a beat.** A tile, a bar, a fill, or the word *agent* moves at least every 2.5 seconds, except the retrieval hold. During that hold the only motion is *agent* drifting six pixels, so the pause stays alive and teaches nothing new.

**Sound.** Warm, close, conversational human voice. Slow the two syllables of *agent* every time it is spoken. A low pulse of music stays ducked under the voice and is muted for the pause. One clamp tick per lock. No tick during the pause.

**Color script.** Charcoal ground the whole way. Slot interior: amber → turf → paper → glass-blue → charcoal with a question mark, which is the opening frame again.

## Hook, open loop, re-hooks, payoff

**Hook (0.0–2.0).** The first frame is the question, spoken and shown together. No logo and no intro. The payoff is withheld: the viewer does not yet know which frame fails.

**Open loop.** “Which agent isn’t a person?” is asked at 0.0. It stays open through the house, the jersey, and the vase, which all keep the person. It closes at 28.4, over the oxidizing image, with “Not a person. A substance at work.” The loop is closed before the ending asks the question again.

**Re-hooks.** Each one is a spoken cliffhanger plus a visual-state change. Gaps between them sit between 5 and 8 seconds.

The retrieval pause (22.4–25.0) sits inside the 18.6 → 25.0 gap, so the hold does not open a hole longer than 8 seconds.

**Retrieval.** From 20.0 the oxidizing frame is on screen and the sentence has been said, but the silhouette is still there. At 22.4 the voice says “Pause.” and stops. For 2.6 seconds the viewer decides: person or not? The rinse at 25.0 is the reveal, after that hold.

**Payoff that loops.** The stack and the brass bar (34.0) state the rule: the sentence is the lock. “Watch it let go” (38.0) proves it — when the bar opens, the pictures leave and *agent* is empty again. By 44.0 the image is the hook. 44.0–46.0 replays the hook line and the hook motion. The last frame equals the first frame, so a replay joins cleanly and the question starts the clamp over.

## Series plan

**Series title:** Clamp, numbered. Clamp #1 is this episode. Later episodes quiz one or two earlier words out loud, as a one-second clamp after that episode’s own hook, before the new word’s first frame. The callback is spoken, not hidden.

## Production approach

**Remotion + GSAP. Difficulty: medium.**

Remotion holds the master clock. The voiceover, the burned-in captions, the clamp ticks, and the GSAP tweens share the timestamps in this board, so the picture and the sentence land on the same frame. GSAP drives the bar overshoot, the slot fills, the rinse, and the one kinetic word. The camera never moves and the props are flat, so a Three.js scene would add lighting and a camera this format does not use. Manim is the wrong tool: this is not a diagram being proved. Medium, because four masked fills, a silent 2.6-second hold that must not drift, and a loop seam at 44.0 have to match frame 0. There is no character rig and no 3D pipeline.

## What this format refuses to be

Clamp refuses to be a definition slide, a hotspot tour of one scene, a role-swap skit, or a myth told and then corrected: the sentence clamp is the whole machine, and the surprise is only the slot that fails the person test.

## Measurement

Score Clamp #1 on the share still watching at 2 seconds, average percentage viewed and where the retention curve dips, plus saves and sends per reach — not on view count.

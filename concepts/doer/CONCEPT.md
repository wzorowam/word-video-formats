# The Shove Test

**Series:** Who Acts #1
**Word:** agent
**Frame:** 9:16 (1080×1920), 30 fps
**Duration:** 48.0 seconds

## Format name and logline

**The Shove Test.** A shove always starts somewhere. A single force ribbon draws from the body that acts to the body that receives, and the origin of that ribbon is the agent — in a sentence, when someone acts for you, and when a thing acts on something else.

## What this format refuses to be

This format refuses to be a myth-then-fact card stack, a sentence blank, a hotspot tour of a room, a duel with agency and agenda, a timeline, a meaning tree, or a terminal.

## Visual system and animation style

Flat vector stage, no scenery to scan. Two geometric people (circle head, rust bob on Maya, stick limbs) and one prop at a time. The only teaching graphic is a persimmon force ribbon (`#E25B2A`), thick at the origin and pointed at the tip. It draws in the same frames the voice says the action. The word **agent** is the only kinetic type: a heavy condensed sans, slammed onto the ribbon’s origin (on the doer, never in a distant corner), with a 6-frame overshoot. Short caption chips sit lower-left, three words or fewer, static, high contrast. They are not the voiceover.

Palette states, each a hard cut:

| State | When | Ground |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PAPER | hook, chunk 1, loop | `#F3EBDD` |
| INK | first re-hook | `#141414`, figures in paper white |
| DUSK | Maya leaves | `#2C3354` |
| WHITE | “who is doing it?” | `#F7F7F5` |
| KITCHEN | soap | `#D7E0D2` |
| QUIZ | retrieval | PAPER plus two pulse rings |
| LOCK | reveal | PAPER, ribbon at full opacity, everything else at 30% |

Safe areas: 140 px clear at the top, 180 px at the bottom. Action lives in the upper 58% of the frame. No logo, no intro card, no lower-third essay.

Animation is hard cuts plus short eases (8–12 frames). A whoosh only when a ribbon launches. A tick only on a re-hook cut. Music is a ducked single-note bed. Voice is warm and conversational, and the syllable of “agent” is given a fraction more time as the stamp lands. Hold the answered image about one second longer than the line.

Frames 0–7 and frames 1432–1439 are the same still: palm an inch from the door, ribbon drawn to 45%, kinetic `AGENT?` at the same scale and position. The ribbon twitch starts at frame 8, so a replay cuts from one still to the same still and then moves. The spoken question finishes as that picture locks.

## Storyboard

Timings sum to 48.0 s. Re-hooks (a new question plus a palette or prop change) land at 8.0, 15.0, 22.0, 29.0, 36.0, and 43.5.

### 0.00–2.00 — Hook (PAPER)

Already mid-shove. Maya’s palm is an inch from a flat door; the ribbon is only half drawn, tip not landed. Kinetic `AGENT?` slams on. No logo.

- 0.00–0.27 the still pose (same 8 frames as the end of the film).
- 0.27–1.40 ribbon twitches forward and stops.
- 1.40–2.00 freeze on the unfinished shove.

### 2.00–8.00 — Wrong belief (PAPER)

- 2.00–5.00 a single pointing finger jabs the door. The door flashes. Chip: “the door?”
- 5.00–8.00 the door slides by itself. The ribbon is stuck on the door with no hand at the origin. Chip: “it moved”.

### 8.00–10.00 — Re-hook (INK)

Hard cut to ink. The finger is gone. A searching dot replaces the missing origin. Chip: “where?”

### 10.00–15.00 — Chunk 1, the shove starts in a person (back to PAPER)

- 10.00–11.40 Maya’s hand enters from the left; the ribbon draws palm → door as the door rocks.
- 11.40–13.40 kinetic `AGENT` stamps onto Maya’s chest, sitting on the ribbon origin.
- 13.40–15.00 hold the answered pair (hand + word + rocked door) so it can stick.

### 15.00–17.00 — Re-hook (DUSK)

Maya steps off the left edge. The door stays. The ribbon’s origin frays. Chip: “no Maya?”

### 17.00–22.00 — Chunk 2, the one who receives is not the agent (DUSK)

- 17.00–19.00 the door slides; the ribbon comes from off the top of the frame.
- 19.00–21.00 `AGENT` tries to stick to the door and slides off onto the floor.
- 21.00–22.00 hold the bare door. The word is not on it. Chip: “lands here”.

### 22.00–24.00 — Re-hook (WHITE)

Hard cut to white. The dotted origin races off the top edge. Chip: “who acts?”

### 24.00–29.00 — Chunk 3, someone who acts for you (WHITE)

- 24.00–26.20 a phone on a table. A booker taps it. The ribbon runs from the booker’s hand into the phone; a ticket slides toward a second figure who never moves.
- 26.20–28.00 `AGENT` stamps the booker, not the seated figure.
- 28.00–29.00 hold: seated figure still, booker labeled. Chip: “books it”.

### 29.00–31.00 — Re-hook (KITCHEN)

The ticket flips into a greasy plate. Ground snaps to kitchen green. Chip: “no person?”

### 31.00–36.00 — Chunk 4, a thing that acts upon (KITCHEN)

- 31.00–33.00 a soap drop falls. The ribbon runs from the drop into the grease; the smear shrinks.
- 33.00–34.80 `AGENT` stamps the drop, not the plate.
- 34.80–36.00 hold the drop + word + shrinking smear. Chip: “on grease”.

### 36.00–38.00 — Re-hook into the quiz (QUIZ)

Props wipe. Maya and the door return, unlabeled. Two pulse rings: one on her hand, one on the door. Chip: “your turn”.

### 38.00–40.50 — Retrieval pause (QUIZ)

Silence for 2.5 s. Rings pulse. Nothing highlights. No new word.

### 40.50–43.50 — Payoff, loop closes (LOCK)

The ribbon locks from Maya’s hand to the door. `AGENT` stamps her chest and stays. The door’s ring dies. Chip: “from her”. Hold the lock through 43.50.

### 43.50–45.50 — Re-hook, one rule (PAPER)

Three small origins — Maya’s hand, the booking hand, the soap drop — flash in a column and collapse into one ribbon. `AGENT` sits on the shared origin. Chip: “if it acts”.

### 45.50–48.00 — Loop back into the hook (PAPER)

The column clears. By 47.20 the frame is the opening shove again: palm an inch from the door, ribbon at 45%, kinetic `AGENT?`. Hold that match through 48.00. A replay is the same picture and the same question.

## Hook, re-hooks, payoff

- **Hook (0–2 s):** an unfinished shove and “Who is the agent?” The answer is not on screen.
- **Open loop:** that question. **Closed at 40.50–43.50** with “Maya. The one it comes from.” and the ribbon locked to her hand.
- **Re-hooks:** 8.0 (where does it start?), 15.0 (what if she isn’t there?), 22.0 (who is doing it?), 29.0 (what if there’s no person?), 36.0 (pause — who?), 43.5 (if it acts). Each one cuts the ground or the prop.
- **Surprise:** the door moves, so it feels like the doer. It isn’t. Movement is where the shove lands. The same test then names a person who books for you and a drop that acts on grease.
- **Loop:** 45.50–48.00 returns to the hook frame and the hook line, after the answer has already been given.

## Series plan — Who Acts

One named, numbered series. The role taught in an episode comes back later as a quiz, not as a new lesson. Episode 1 never teaches a second target word; the door is only “not the agent.” Later episodes name the other roles.

1. **agent** — Who Acts #1, this film. The origin of the shove.
2. **patient** — Who Acts #2. The door gets a name: the one the shove lands on. Cold open quizzes #1: Maya or the door — who was the agent?
3. **instrument** — Who Acts #3. The key in the hand is neither doer nor receiver. Opens by quizzing agent and patient.
4. **beneficiary** — Who Acts #4. The friend who eats the meal someone else booked. Quizzes the agent who booked, and the instrument.
5. **experiencer** — Who Acts #5. Someone flinches at the slam but does not shove. Quizzes agent and patient.

## Production

**Remotion**, 1080×1920, 30 fps, one composition (48.0 s = 1440 frames). The ribbon is a stroked path whose dash offset is tied to the frame. Kinetic type is a spring on the spoken syllable. The hook pose is one component on frames 0–7 and frames 1432–1439, so the seam is the same still.

**Difficulty: medium.** The picture has to stay glued to the syllable, the ribbon origin has to move with a hand, and the seam has to match. It does not need a 3D camera, a simulation, or a character acting cycle — limb rotation and a translation of the door are enough. Three.js would add a camera the lesson never uses. Manim is built for math constructions, not this stage. A separate GSAP page would fight the frame-locked voice track, so this stays Remotion only.

## Measurement

Score hook hold (share still watching at 2 s), average percent viewed, the retention curve, engaged-view rate, and saves and sends per reach; a replay bump on the seam means the loop is working. Ignore raw view counts, and do not ask people to share.

## Checklist

1. **Hook in 0–2 s with a payoff or a curiosity gap.** Met. 0.00–2.00 opens on the unfinished shove and “Who is the agent?” No logo, no intro.
2. **Open a loop and close it at the end.** Met. The question is asked at 0.00 and answered at 40.50–43.50 (“Maya. The one it comes from.”), before the ending re-opens it for the replay.
3. **Re-hook every 5–8 s with a visual-state change.** Met. Cuts at 8.0 (ink), 15.0 (dusk, Maya exits), 22.0 (white), 29.0 (kitchen plate), 36.0 (quiz rings), and 43.5 (three origins collapse). Gaps: 8, 7, 7, 7, 7, 7.5 s. Inside the longer beats the picture also changes about every 2–3 s (finger, sliding door, ribbon draw, stamp, slide-off).
4. **One word, 3–5 chunks, nothing extra.** Met. Four chunks of agent only: starts in Maya (10.00–15.00), does not land on the door (17.00–22.00), acts for you (24.00–29.00), acts upon the grease (31.00–36.00). No second target word, no etymology, no agency/agenda.
5. **Narration synced to the animation.** Met. The script table pairs every line with the picture at that moment: the ribbon draws on the verb, and `AGENT` stamps or slides off on the spoken word. The label sits on the doer.
6. **Kinetic type for the target word only.** Met. Only `AGENT` is large and animated. Chips are three words or fewer. The voiceover is not printed on screen. Sound-off, the ribbon origin plus the stamp plus the chip still read.
7. **Short captions, word repeated in context.** Met. Same-language chips are burned in on the beats above. “Agent” is spoken in context at 0.00, 11.40, 19.00, 24.00, 33.00, 36.40, 44.20, and 45.50, slower on the word itself.
8. **Retrieval pause of 2–3 s before the reveal.** Met. Prompt at 36.00–38.00, silence 38.00–40.50 (2.5 s), reveal at 40.50.
9. **Concrete surprise.** Met. 2.00–8.00 points at the moving door; 17.00–22.00 and 40.50 show that the mover is the wrong body. The booking (24.00) and the soap (31.00) put the same test in ordinary actions.
10. **Narrative template.** Met. Hook question 0.00–2.00 → wrong belief 2.00–8.00 → twist and four chunks with re-hooks 8.00–36.00 → quiz 36.00–40.50 → payoff 40.50–45.50 → loop line 45.50–48.00.
11. **Named numbered series with spaced quizzes.** Met. Who Acts #1–#5. #2 quizzes agent; #3 quizzes agent and patient; #4 quizzes agent and instrument; #5 quizzes agent and patient.
12. **Measure rates, not views.** Met. One note in Measurement, above. Not a visual beat.
